## Landmarks: what the court's dimensions back up

- **Where you stop at the net: level with the second post, about 2 m from the net.** The same landmark as Where to stand, counting the net post as the first (16: 2 m fence panels put posts 2 m and 4 m from the net). Nothing in this research contradicts it. Source 14 also tells players to use the mesh posts as markers. Note for the scenes: the Opponents lob you scenes stand net players about 2.8 m back (y ≈ 12.8); stopping at y = 12 matches the landmark.
- **The gap you don't wait in: between your service line and the second post.** The service line is 6.95 m from the net (15), so the gap is about 5 m long. Source 1's "no man's land" is exactly this, if it counts posts as we do (it doesn't say). Source 14's version, from the service line to about 3 m from the net, agrees to within a metre.
- **Where the lob lands: between their service line and their back glass,** a strip about 3 m deep (15: the service line is 3.05 m from the back wall). Source 4 uses this target, and so does the Returning serve page.
- **Where you start: level with the glass join,** 2 m from the back wall (16), inside source 7's 2–3 m.
- Not used: the end of the side glass. It is 6 m from the net, just under a metre in front of the service line (16), so it could mark the back of the gap on the side fence, but the service line is on the floor and easier to see.

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **Every lob, or only a good one?** Source 7 says every lob should send both players two or three steps forward ("a lob without advancing is a wasted opportunity"). Sources 9/10/12, 11 and 13 say stay back when the lob is short or doesn't push them back. Source 6, from the same site as 7, says advance "after a good lob". **We chose: go up only when the lob gets over them.** It's what most sources say, it's what 6 says too, and a beginner caught halfway up by a smash can't recover.
- **All the way to the net, or a step at a time?** Sources 9/12 say once the lob beats them, go up to the net; source 3 says the lob gives both of you time to reach an attacking position. Source 6 says move up in small steps: two or three after a good lob, another after a low ball at their feet, the rest after a weak reply. Source 8 says there's no fixed spot; it depends on how good your ball was. These fit together once the shot is taken into account. **We chose: after a high, deep lob, the beginner scene goes all the way to the second post, because that lob buys the time. After a chiquita there's less time, so the advanced scene stops short, split steps as they hit, and closes in on the next ball.**
- **Where "no man's land" is.** Source 1: between the second post and the service line. Source 14: from the service line to about 3 m from the net. Source 6: vaguely "in between" (behind the service line is defence, inside the service box is the net). Source 1 says good players often end up there, because they keep moving while the ball travels; source 8's own example, after a less good low ball, stops just past the service line. **We chose "the gap between the service line and the second post": pass through it, don't wait in it.** With our post counting it matches source 1 and is within a metre of source 14. The advanced scene shows the exception: a split step inside the gap, then straight on in.
- **Is the chiquita for beginners?** Source 1 lists it with the lobs as one of three ways forward for everyone. Source 7 calls it an intermediate tool; sources 12 and 13 call it the hardest option, and 12 says it usually leads to a volley exchange that could go either way. **We chose: the chiquita goes on the Advanced tab; beginners get the lob.**
- **Level with your partner, or the diagonal player a little deeper?** Source 8 says after a lob the player on the diagonal stays slightly behind their partner, to cover the lob back. Source 6 says keep both players at the same depth; source 2 says move together. **We chose: level, in both scenes.** It's the majority, it's simpler to see, and a one-source nuance that another source contradicts doesn't meet our rule.
- **Their weak smash.** Source 8 says you can move forward and volley it to take the net; source 6 says hold your ground and drive it low through the middle. **Not used:** this page's ways up are your lob and your chiquita. The smash belongs with Opponents lob you and the Bandeja page.
- **The passing shot and the wall exit.** Sources 9/10/12 list a ball driven past them (off the back glass, down the side) as a third way up, if it doesn't bounce far off their glass. Sources 1, 3, 4 and 7 don't. **Not used:** the site has no page for it, and it's the riskier ball. The page shows the lob and the chiquita, which have shot pages.

## Proposed plays and scenes

Coordinates are the engine's: x across the court from 0 (the left side wall as you face the net) to 10; y along it from 0 (their back wall) to 20 (yours). The net is at y = 10, your service line at y = 16.95 and theirs at y = 3.05, the second posts at y = 12 and y = 8, the glass joins at y = 18 and y = 2. "You" play on the right. Both scenes start with you and your partner back and the opponents at the net, which is where the path leaves you after Returning serve and Ball off the back glass.

Proposed advice for every level (from the table):

- Go up only once your shot has worked: your lob is over them, or your low ball is at their feet. Otherwise stay back.
- Go together and stay level. One up and one back leaves the middle open.
- Stop as they're about to hit, and split step. Get as close as the time allows: level with the second post, about 2 m from the net, if you can.
- Don't wait between the service line and the second post. Go through it, or go back.
- At the net, you're in place, not yet attacking. Finish only an easy ball.

### Beginner: lob deep, then go up together

- **The play:** a high, deep, cross-court lob. Only when it's clearly over them do you both walk up to the net, stop as they hit, and volley their reply back deep. If the lob drops short, stay back.
- **Shots:** Lob, then Volley.
- **Sketch (about 7.5 s):**
  - t 0: you at (7.3, 18.0) and your partner at (2.7, 18.0), level with the glass join. Opponents at the net: opp1 at (3.0, 7.8), opp2 at (7.0, 7.8).
  - t 0.4: opp2 volleys deep to you. You let it bounce (it can come off the glass, as in step 3).
  - t 1.6: you lob high (peak about 7 m) and cross-court, over opp1, to land around (2.8, 1.6): beyond their service line, before their glass. Light up their service line. It lands at about t 3.9.
  - t 2.0–4.8: opp1 turns and runs back to play it after the bounce; opp2 drops back level with them.
  - **Decision, t ≈ 2.6**, as the lob passes over opp1 and they turn: "Your lob is over them. Stay back, or go up?"
  - t 2.7–4.9: you and your partner go up together, level, to about (6.8, 12.0) and (3.2, 12.0), about 6.5 m at a jog (2.5–3 m/s). Light up the second post. Both split step at about t 4.9 as opp1 hits.
  - t 5.0: opp1, deep, sends a slow, mid-height ball towards you.
  - t 6.1: you volley it deep, back to their end (around (4.0, 3.0)). No winner: you've got the net, and keeping it is the job.
  - Caption detail to carry the other half: "Had the lob dropped short, you'd both have stayed back."

### Advanced: chiquita at their feet, then close in

- **The play:** when a ball comes off your glass at a comfortable height and they're tight on the net, drop a chiquita at the feet of the diagonal player. As it dips, both of you move in fast, split step as they scoop it up, and volley their lifted ball down. Go only as far as the chiquita buys you; close the rest on the next ball.
- **Shots:** Chiquita, then Volley.
- **Sketch (about 7 s):**
  - t 0: you at (7.3, 18.2) and your partner at (2.7, 18.2). Opponents at the net: opp1 at (3.0, 7.6), opp2 at (7.0, 7.6).
  - t 0.3: opp1 volleys deep cross-court; it bounces on your side and comes off your back glass at a comfortable height.
  - t 1.6: you play a chiquita from about (7.6, 18.8): soft, peak under 2 m, just clearing the net and dipping to land about 2 m past it, at opp1's feet around (3.4, 7.9). Flight about 1.3–1.5 s.
  - **Decision, t ≈ 2.5**, as the chiquita dips at opp1's feet: "It's at their feet. Stay back, or go in?"
  - t 1.8–2.9: you and your partner move in fast together (about 3.5 m/s) to around y = 14.8, inside the gap between the service line and the second post. Both split step as opp1 is about to hit.
  - t 2.9: opp1 has to lift it: a soft, rising ball towards the middle, around (6.2, 13.2).
  - t 3.0–4.0: you step in and take it early as a volley at about (6.6, 13.4), hitting down at their feet or through the middle. Your partner comes up level.
  - t 4.0–5.5: both close in to the second post, (6.8, 12.0) and (3.2, 12.0), and light it up. Their next ball is defensive; the scene ends with you holding the net.
  - Caption detail for "When not to": "If the chiquita sits up, stay back. They'll volley it at your feet."
