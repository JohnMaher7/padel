## Landmarks: what the court's dimensions back up

- **The centre service line and the "T".** Each half has a centre service line running from the net to the service line, down the middle of the court, 5 m from each side wall (22, 23). The service line is 6.95 m from the net (22). So the line is a real target: at the net, aim along it; for a deep volley, aim just past their T, where it meets their service line. Behind the service line there is only a short stub (see "differ" below), so the back part of the middle has no painted landmark. The page should say "the middle of their back glass" there, not a glass panel or join: the data sheets we have don't fix how the back glass is divided.
- **The side walls.** The middle is 5 m from both side walls, the most room from both. That matters because of a rule: a ball that hits their wall or fence before it bounces is our point lost (22, Rule 13 g).
- **The net.** Lowest in the middle, 0.88 m against 0.92 m at the posts (22, 23). The difference is real but small; use it at most in a step's `detail`.

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **Who takes a ball down the middle.** Source 7 says usually, but not always, the player with the forehand in the middle. Source 13 says it depends on the ball: a defensive ball goes to whoever it's coming towards; a mid-height volley can be the cross-court player's forehand or the straight player's backhand, agreed in advance; a lob goes to the left-side player, on the forehand. Source 12, for the back of the court, says most middle balls belong to the player diagonal to the hitter, because the ball runs towards them off the back glass. **We chose "agree it before you play; the usual default is the forehand in the middle"** (the left-side player, for two right-handers). It's what most sources say, it's simple enough for every level, and source 13's and 12's refinements belong in a later "Both teams at the net" topic. In both scenes, the opponent who takes the ball, and our partner who covers the middle, is the one with the forehand there.
- **At the net only, or from the back too.** Sources 3 and 18 put the middle under net play; 17 makes it the default for volleys and overheads. Sources 11 and 19 also use it from the back when both opponents are at the net, and 15 builds rallies through the centre from the back. Source 6 warns that a lob into the middle is the easiest to smash, and 13 says why (it meets the left player's forehand). 17 says the middle is "especially" right "on lobs and defensive blocks from deep", which we read as when you're answering their lobs and blocks, not lobbing into the middle yourself, so it doesn't contradict 6. **We chose: at the net, the middle is your default; from the back, only a low ball at their feet, never a slow, high lob into the middle.** Every source's advice holds under those conditions, and nothing contradicts them.
- **When you're stretched or late.** Source 11 says to play it straight (down the line), because that's the natural stroke from a difficult position. Source 21 says a controlled ball to the middle beats going for a line from an awkward position, and 18 says "when in doubt", go down the middle. **We chose the middle as the default when in doubt**: two sources against one, it fits the LTA's "safest" framing (1), and the rule about their walls backs it. Neither scene shows a stretched ball, so this reaches the page only as the advice line; revisit it on the Volley shot page.
- **Beginners at the net: middle or cross-court.** Source 11 calls cross-court the beginner's direction, because it's the longest line; sources 1, 17 and 18 recommend the middle to beginners. Source 11 is mostly about hitting from the back, and Returning serve already teaches cross-court there. **We chose the middle for the Beginner play**, because it's at the net, three sources back it for that level, and it has the same "room for error" argument.
- **How deep and how hard.** Source 4 wants the middle volley a bit slower, so the ball dies near their back glass; 2 wants push volleys to land past the service line; 16 and 20 aim at the feet when the opponents are at the net. This is a condition, not a conflict: **deep and unhurried when they're at the back, low at their feet when they're at the net.** Both our scenes have them at the back, so both volleys go deep.
- **Where to finish once they've moved to the middle.** Source 17 says go wide; 3 says the middle opens wider angles for the next shot; 4 says the corner or the double glass, with more pace, on a higher ball; 9 says the fence (the side mesh), on the right ball, because its bounce is the least predictable. **We chose an angled volley into the open side, landing a few metres past the net so it runs on into their side mesh.** It's where "go wide" (17, 3) and "the fence" (9) meet, and it shows the space their move to the middle left.
- **The centre line's stub.** FIP says the centre service line carries on at least 20 cm past the service line (22); the LTA data sheet says 10 cm (23). It doesn't matter for us: the anchor is the line and the T, which both have.

## Proposed plays and scenes

Coordinates are in metres, as the scenes use them: x runs 0–10 across the court (x 10 is the right side wall as you look at the net from our end), y runs 0–20 from their back wall (y 0) through the net (y 10) to ours (y 20). Their service line is at y 3.05, ours at y 16.95, and the centre service line is at x 5. "You" play on the right, as everywhere on the site. Both plays are at the net: in the Start here story you've just taken the net (step 4), and they lob you next (step 6).

### Beginner

- **Play:** At the net, volley deep down the middle.
- **Shots:** Volley.
- **Why it's the right play at this level:** it's the biggest target on the court, 5 m from both walls, and it asks nothing clever of you. The doubt and the weak reply come free.
- **Where everyone stands (t = 0):** you at the net on the right (≈ 7.0, 12.4) and your partner on the left (≈ 3.0, 12.4), both about 2–2.5 m from the net and a touch in from the middle of your halves. The opponents are at the back: their right-side player at (≈ 3.0, 1.8), diagonal from you, and their left-side player at (≈ 7.0, 1.8), straight across from you, with the forehand towards the middle.
- **The rally that sets it up (t ≈ 1.0):** their right-side player drives a medium-height ball cross-court to you. It reaches you at about chest height (≈ t 2.0): not a ball you can put away, not one at your feet.
- **The shot (t ≈ 2.0):** you volley along the centre line, unhurried, low over the net. It lands just past their T (≈ 5.0, 2.6) and is dying as it reaches their back glass.
- **What it causes (t ≈ 2.4–4.0):** both opponents turn to the middle. Their right-side player steps in, then checks; their left-side player takes it late, from the middle of the back of the court, and can only lift a soft ball straight back through the middle (towards ≈ 4.8, 12.8). You and your partner each shade a step towards the middle while it's in the air.
- **Follow-up (t ≈ 4.8):** your partner, with the forehand in the middle, volleys it deep down the middle again. End at about 6.5 s.
- **Decision moment (t ≈ 2.0):** as the drive comes to you: "It's coming to you at the net. Where do you aim?" The answering caption starts at the same time: "Deep, down the middle."
- **Caption sketch:** "You've both taken the net." / "Deep, down the middle." / "Whose is it? They both hesitate." / "The reply comes back to the middle."
- **Watch out for (sources 4, 5):** hitting it hard or high. A ball that comes off their back glass fast, or sits up off it, gives them an easy one; the aim is a ball that's dying as it gets there.

### Advanced

- **Play:** Middle first, then angle it into the gap.
- **Shots:** Volley.
- **Why it's the right play at this level:** it takes the initiative without a risky first ball. The first volley is safe; it's the second, into the space they left, that wins the point.
- **Where everyone stands (t = 0):** as in the Beginner scene: you (≈ 7.0, 12.4), partner (≈ 3.0, 12.4), opponents at (≈ 3.0, 1.8) and (≈ 7.0, 1.8).
- **The rally that sets it up (t ≈ 1.0):** their right-side player drives cross-court to you, reaching you at about chest height at t ≈ 2.0.
- **The shot (t ≈ 2.0):** you volley deep down the middle, just past their T.
- **What it causes (t ≈ 2.4–3.8):** both opponents pull into the middle, ending up about a metre either side of the centre line (≈ 4.3 and ≈ 5.8, y ≈ 2.0). Their left-side player takes it and lifts a soft, central ball back to you, to your side of the middle (≈ 6.2, 12.5), arriving at t ≈ 4.8. Both of their sides are now empty.
- **Decision moment (t ≈ 4.8):** as the soft reply reaches you: "They're both in the middle. Where now?" Answering caption at the same time: "Now angle it into the gap."
- **The finish (t ≈ 4.8):** a backhand volley angled cross-court, short and wide: it lands about 3.5 m past the net (≈ 1.2, 6.5), away from both of them, and runs on into their side mesh (around y 5). The nearest opponent is deep in the middle and has about 5 m to run, forwards and wide. End at about 7.5 s.
- **Caption sketch:** "You've both taken the net." / "First, deep down the middle." / "They both close in on it." / "Now angle it into the gap."
- **When not to (sources 4, 9):** if the first ball is high and easy, don't bother with the middle; angle it away straight away. If their reply comes back low, at your feet, go down the middle again rather than forcing the angle.
- **Watch out for (sources 10, 11, 14, 19):** a pair with a left-hander on the right has two forehands in the middle, so play wide against them; and don't go down the middle every time, or they'll wait for it.

### Considered, not proposed: the middle from the back (Chiquita)

Sources 8, 11, 16, 19 and 20 support a version from the back: you and your partner are back (y ≈ 18), both opponents at the net (≈ 3.0 and ≈ 7.0, y ≈ 7.8); they volley a soft, easy ball to you; you play a slow, low chiquita between them, landing at their feet (≈ 5.0, 7.5); both hesitate, one volleys it up, and you both walk in to the net. Its decision moment would be the easy ball coming to you at the back. It's a good pattern, but its point is taking the net, which is step 4 ("When to take the net"); source 8 frames the chiquita exactly that way. We propose using it there, or on the Chiquita shot page, and giving this topic the "from the back, keep it low" advice line only.
