## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **How far off the net the opponents must be.** Most sources say not tight to the net (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11). Source 6 gives a number: at least 2–3 m. Source 1 says it's time "when they're about at that second post or maybe just in front", and source 2 says good lobs keep them "almost at that second post". Two weaker sources say the opposite: "chosen when net players are too close" (14) and "close to the net" (13). Source 3 adds an outer limit: if they're standing well back, it reaches them at a comfortable height. Source 2 disagrees that this is bad: if they step back and let it bounce, you've still made space. **We chose:** play it when they're level with the second post or further back (at least about 2 m of space in front of them). If they're closer than that, lob instead. **Owner to check:** our site counts the net post as the first post, so the second post is about 2 m from the net ("Where to stand"). The Padel School's "second post" may count differently. Source 6's 2–3 m suggests they mean a bit further back.
- **Spin.** Flat is the default for 1, 4, 6, 7 and 11. Topspin is "ideal but harder" (7), preferred by advanced players (3), or allowed when the ball hasn't come off the wall (11). Heavy topspin is discouraged by 1, 2 and 10. Slice: source 5 says the backhand slice is easier, and source 12 slices a high "push" chiquita, but sources 7 and 11 warn that slice floats and gives them time. **We chose:** flat with a slightly open face. Topspin is an advanced note. We don't recommend slice.
- **Swing direction.** Source 3 says low to high, brushing up the back of the ball. Source 10 calls it "like a small lob", hitting under the ball. Source 7 says hit forward, *not* upward. Sources 6 and 12 say "push" it. **We chose:** start with the racket below the ball and push gently forward and a little up, "a small lob" and not a scoop. The legs give the lift (4, 7).
- **Height over the net.** Sources 3 and 4 say "just over the net". Source 2 says clear it by "a good foot": an inch or two is too risky, and the slow arc does the work. **We chose:** source 2's safer margin, because it's a beginner-friendly page and nothing says a foot is too high when the ball is slow.
- **Where to aim.** Source 3: the feet on their backhand side, or the centre. Source 11: cross-court through the middle. Source 12: at whichever volleyer is slower to step in. Source 13: "dead" near the net. **We chose:** at the feet, with the middle as the default. The "near the net" idea is left out, because it contradicts "at their feet" when they're 2 m or more back.
- **Forehand or backhand.** Source 5 says the backhand is often preferred. The others say both. **We chose:** both, with no preference.
- **Contact height.** Source 3 says knee to waist. Source 12 has a second version: a push with slice, taken above the waist when a bad overhead bounces high. **We chose:** the standard version (after the glass, dropping, knee to waist). The high push is left out.
