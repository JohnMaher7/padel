The step list these support (to be reworded on the page):

1. Get ready early: continental grip, racket back, turn side-on.
2. Get low: set your feet and bend your knees so you're below the ball.
3. Open the racket face and drop the racket below the ball.
4. Meet the ball in front of you, around waist height or lower, underneath it, with a firm grip.
5. Swing smoothly up through the ball, lifting with your legs, not your wrist.
6. Finish high, towards your target, without cutting the swing short.
7. Aim deep and usually cross-court, then move up to the net with your partner.

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **Spin.** Source 14 says the padel lob is hit flat under the ball with a vertical racket path, not brushed with topspin as in tennis; source 9 says prepare below the ball to avoid an unwanted slice. Source 10 calls a flat lob with a little backspin the safe version and topspin the risky one. Source 12 suggests a little backspin for higher-level players, so the ball stays close to the glass. We teach the flat lob under the ball, and mention a touch of backspin only as an advanced extra.
- **How deep.** Source 5 says bounce past the service line but before the back glass; source 11 says the deep corner past the service line; source 10 says between the service line and the back glass, peaking about 3–4 m up; source 7 says near the back line, or over them to hit the back glass high. We chose "bounce between their service line and the back glass, the deeper the better", which all of them fit.
- **Direction.** Cross-court is everyone's default (3, 5, 7, 10, 13). Sources 3 and 13 add that down the line is hard to smash but hard to hit (the shortest line), and that the middle is where the opponent finds it easiest to attack off the glass, so a lob there should be the fast one. We give cross-court as the rule, and the other two as advanced options.
- **Always follow the lob in?** Source 7 says every lob should trigger both players to take two or three steps forward. Source 15 says move in if the lob has cleared them, but if it's short, or they smash well, stay back and defend for another chance. We chose source 15's version: move in behind a deep lob that gets over them; stay back after a short one.
- **Defensive or attacking?** Sources 5, 6 and 7 present the lob as both a way out of defence and an attack. Sources 13, 14 and 16 insist it's mainly an attacking shot, played on a ball you're comfortable with, because a lob played under pressure usually lands short. We present it as the main way to go from defence to the net, played on a ball you can control; when you're stretched, keep the ball in play another way.
