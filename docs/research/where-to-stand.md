## Landmarks: what the court's dimensions back up

- **The glass join.** The side glass is two 2 m panels, so the join between them is 2 m from the back wall (12, "variant 1": a first step 3 m high by 2 m long, then a second 2 m high by 2 m long). The service line is 3.05 m from the back wall, so the join is about a metre behind it. Most courts in the UK are built this way (12). A court with one long 4 m glass panel on each side has no join, so the page gives the distance too.
- **The posts.** Side fence panels are 2 m wide (13: mesh panels of 2000 × 3000 mm, some with a door built in), so posts stand 2 m and 4 m from the net, and the glass starts 6 m from it. Courts also have one or two openings next to the net (11, 12: each 0.72–1.10 m wide when there are two), and makers place their panels differently around them. The page says "about 2 m" as well as "the second post", and tells the reader to go by the distance if their club's posts don't match.

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **How far from the net the net player stands, and which post marks it.** This is where the sources disagree most.
  - By distance: 1–1.5 m (4), 1–2 m (3), "two or three steps" for beginners (7).
  - By post: "horizontally in line with the second post on the side fence" (1, for where the server ends up after moving in); "aligned with the second post" (2); between the first and second posts, and never behind the second (7, 8).
  - Source 2 counts posts from the court's entrance and says the third is where the mesh meets the glass. On a court with 2 m panels, that puts its second post 4 m from the net, deeper than every distance source.
  - **We chose "level with the second post, about 2 m from the net", counting the net post as the first.** On a court with 2 m panels that's the first fence post back from the net. It's where the distance sources (1–2 m) and the post sources (1, 7, 8) overlap, and it's the owner's own reference. The page gives the distance with the landmark, because post spacing and counting vary. **Owner to check:** how your club's posts are spaced, and whether counting from the net post puts the second post about 2 m back.
- **The receiver: how far from the side glass.** Source 2 says about 2 m; source 3 says near the centre of the receiving area (about 2.5 m); others say "near the side wall". We chose about 2 m, which matches source 2's landmark (the first join in the back glass), and drew the receiver a little further in, where the serve can't cramp them.
- **The receiver's partner: back, or at the net.** Sources 2, 3, 4 and 6 say back, level with the receiver. The Returning serve research found coaches who move advanced players up to the service line. This page has one play for every level, so it shows the start everyone can use: back, level with the receiver. Returning serve's Advanced tab shows the other option.
- **Which side your partner serves from.** Serves alternate sides every point (11). "You" play on the right throughout, so in chapter 2 your partner serves from the left, as on any even point, and you're at the net on the right.
