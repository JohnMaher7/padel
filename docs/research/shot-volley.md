The step list these support (to be reworded on the page):

1. Ready: continental grip, racket up in front of you, knees bent, on your toes.
2. Split step as they hit.
3. Turn your shoulders; the racket goes back only as far as your shoulder.
4. Step in with the opposite foot, moving your weight forward.
5. Meet the ball in front of you with a firm wrist and the racket head up; for a low ball, bend your knees.
6. Block or punch, with no big swing, and finish short towards your target.
7. Get straight back to ready. Aim low (at their feet or down the middle) and deep, not hard.

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **Which foot to step with.** The basic teaching (1, 4, 8, 9, 10) is to step with the opposite foot. Sources 11 and 12 add that on a fast ball it's fine to take a small step with the same-side foot, because it's quicker and easier to recover from, as long as you still turn your shoulders. Source 14 prefers not to "step" on purpose: stand leaning forward and let yourself fall onto the correct leg as the ball comes. We teach the opposite-foot step as the default, and say that with no time a small step on the same side is fine if the shoulders still turn.
- **Slice or flat.** Source 4's attacking "fizz" volley and source 5's control volley use slice; source 14 starts every volley with some slice, pushed back to front rather than chopped down. Source 15 wants volleys flatter, with only a little backspin and a face that isn't too open; sources 8 and 9 list slicing everything as a mistake and say flat gives more control. We teach a firm, nearly flat face with a short forward punch; a touch of underspin comes naturally, and there's no chopping down.
- **Racket position when ready.** Sources 1 and 10 say upright in front of you; 5, 7 and 9 say chest height; 4 says at the height you'll hit it, above the net. These fit together: racket in front, head up, around chest height.
- **How far from the net.** Source 6 says 3–4 m; source 14 says 2–2.5 m, between the first and second post; source 11 says between halfway to the second post and the second post itself. Post spacing varies between courts, so we use the landmark from "Where to stand" (level with the second post, about 2 m back) and add: step in when the ball is dropping at their feet, back when a lob is coming.
- **Where to aim a neutral volley.** Source 13 says play a neutral volley back where it came from, so your pair keeps its shape, and don't change direction off a neutral ball from the line. Sources 5, 6 and 12 say aim low, at the feet; source 14 says the corners. We give "low, deep and at their feet or down the middle" as the default, and "back where it came from" when you're under pressure.
- **Finish low or towards the target.** Source 14 finishes low, towards the opposite pocket; source 15 goes high to low; source 5 finishes towards the target. These don't conflict: a short finish forward and a little down.
