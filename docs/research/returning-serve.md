| Racket back before the ball bounces | 3 | Advice; beginner play |

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **Where the returner stands.** Source 4 says about a metre behind the service line; source 5 says close to the back glass; source 3 says "one small step back" from the baseline. We went with source 4 because it's the most specific, and it lines up with a landmark: the join between the two side glass panels is 2 m from the back wall, and the service line is 3.05 m from it. The owner suggested using the glass join as the anchor.
- **Where the returner's partner stands.** Source 2 recommends back, level with the returner; sources 4 and 5 say at the net or on the service line, "depending on level". So we split it by level: back for beginners, on the service line for advanced players, who move in behind a lob.
- **Lob or low return for beginners.** Source 5 says beginners should stick to consistent cross-court returns and add lobs later; source 4 calls the lob underrated in club padel. We gave beginners the low block to the server (simplest, lowest risk) and the lob to the Advanced tab.
