## Landmarks: what the court's dimensions back up

- **The glass join, for "give it room".** The side glass is two 2 m panels, so the join between them is 2 m from the back glass (18, "Variant 1": a first step 3 m high by 2 m long, then 2 m high by 2 m long). That matches the room the coaches ask for (1.5–2 m, and "at least 2 m" for a fast ball, 6). So: for a fast ball, be about level with the glass join when you hit it. Courts with one long 4 m panel on each side (18, "Variant 2 Crystal") have no join, so the page gives the distance too. The engine already has the `glass-join` highlight.
- **The back wall.** 3 m of glass, then 1 m of mesh, 4 m in all (18, "Ends"). A lob that hits the glass high drops steeply and short (6); a ball into the mesh dies (9). Neither is in these scenes.
- **The service line** is 3.05 m from the back glass. Source 11 says to wait between the service line and the back glass, and for a very fast ball even in front of the service line. Not used as a landmark: the glass join says the same thing more simply.

## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **At what height to hit it.** Around the waist (11; 1 says get low and swing up); the highest point, just as it starts to drop (10; 6 says "the highest comfortable point"); chest or shoulder height when you want power (12). The chiquita wants knee to waist (8). **We chose: let it come off and drop to about waist height, in front of you.** It's where both the lob and the chiquita are easiest, and it's the height that rewards waiting instead of rushing. A ball that comes out higher is a chance to attack (10, 12, 13, 15), which we leave for later.
- **How far from the glass.** 1.5–2 m of room (6, 9); at least 2 m for a fast ball (6); much closer for a slow one (1, 12); in front of the service line for a very fast one (11). **We chose about 2 m, level with the glass join, for a fast ball, and say "closer if it's slow".** It's where the numbers overlap, and it's a landmark you can see.
- **The Beginner shot.** A lob (5, 7, 9, 13); "difficult balls flat, easy balls as lobs" (14); a lob if you're comfortable, otherwise a safe, lower ball (12). **We chose the lob.** Most sources give it, it's one of our four shots, and with both opponents at the net it's the one shot that also pushes them back. The Beginner play can add 14's point: if you're cramped and can't get under it, just get it back over.
- **The Advanced shot.** Soft and low to the feet (5, 8, 10, 13); hard through the middle or at the body when the ball comes out high (10, 12, 13, 15); the bajada (6). **We chose the chiquita.** It's one of our four shots, it works off the usual knee-to-waist ball, and every source that gives it pairs it with moving forward, which leads into step 4. Hitting hard down the middle is step 5's topic, and the bajada isn't a launch shot.
- **Where the lob should land.** Between their service line and back glass, high enough to keep it off their glass (16); near the back line or high off their back glass (7); into their back glass (9). **We chose 16's** (a federation's coaching page, and the same choice as the Returning serve research): deep, between their service line and back glass.
- **What to do after the lob.** Follow it in to the net (7; the "Opponents lob you" Beginner play does this); get back to your spot with your partner (6). **We first chose to get back level with your partner**, leaving the move up to step 4. **Changed (2026-09-27):** step 4's research found that coaches send you up once your lob has clearly beaten them, and "Opponents lob you" already does this, so the Beginner scene now ends with both of you walking up behind the lob. Step 4 explains when to go and when not to.

## Proposed plays and scenes

Court coordinates as the scenes use them: x runs 0–10 across the court (x = 10 is the right side wall as you look towards the net from our end); our back glass is at y = 20, the glass join at y = 18, our service line at y = 16.95, the net at y = 10, their service line at y = 3.05. "You" play on the right, as in the other Start here steps.

**The same incoming ball for both levels**, as in Returning serve and Opponents lob you, so the viewer can compare the two answers:

- **Where everyone stands.** You about (6.8, 17.8) and your partner about (3.2, 17.8), both back, about a metre behind the service line. Both opponents at the net: opp1 about (3.3, 7.8), opp2 about (6.8, 7.9). That's where the point has got to after the serve and return.
- **The ball that gets past you.** Opp2 (straight in front of you) volleys firmly down the line, deep: it lands about (7.8, 17.7), just to your right, and goes past your hip. The opening shot can be this volley, with `from` at opp2's racket at about t = 0.8.
- **Off the glass.** It hits the back glass at about (8.0, 20), a metre or so up, and comes back out almost along the same line (a straight ball comes out straight), about 2 m, dropping.
- **Where you move.** Turn side-on (shoulder to the right side glass), shuffle back and a little to the left, out of the ball's line, to about (7.1, 18.5). Let it pass, then step forward into it and hit it at about waist height with the ball about (8.0, 18.2): level with the glass join, ball on your forehand, in front of you. Highlight the `glass-join` as the caption says "give it room".
- **Your partner** stays back, level with you, and moves across a little to cover the middle.

### Beginner

- **The play:** turn, give it room, let it come off and drop, then lob it cross-court, high and deep. Then get back level with your partner.
- **Shot:** Lob.
- **The scene (about 7 s):**
  1. t ≈ 0: "They're both at the net." Opp2 volleys it deep, past you.
  2. **Decision, at t ≈ 1.4, as it bounces past you:** "It's past you, heading for the glass. What do you do?" The wrong instinct is to chase it into the corner.
  3. Turn and back off out of its line, to the glass join. It hits the glass and comes out.
  4. t ≈ 2.3: you lob it cross-court from about (7.2, 18.2) to land about (3.0, 2.3), between their service line and back glass, peak about 7 m. It goes over opp1, the net player diagonally across.
  5. Both opponents turn and run back to chase it (it lands at t ≈ 5). You step back to your spot, about (6.8, 17.6), level with your partner. The last caption can point to step 4.

### Advanced

- **The play:** the same read and the same room, then a soft chiquita cross-court, dipping at the feet of the net player diagonally across, and move in together while they volley it up.
- **Shot:** Chiquita.
- **The scene (about 7 s):**
  1. The same start, the same volley and the same footwork as the Beginner scene.
  2. **Decision, at t ≈ 2.0, as it comes off the glass:** "It's come off at knee height, and they're both at the net. What do you play?"
  3. t ≈ 2.3: you play it soft, cross-court, low over the net, to land at opp1's feet, about (3.8, 8.2), on their backhand side (towards the middle). Keep the arc as low as the engine allows: it has no topspin, so the ball will fly a little higher than a real chiquita.
  4. Opp1 has to volley from below the net, so it goes up: a slow ball back to about (5.5, 15), peak about 3 m.
  5. Meanwhile you and your partner walk in together, side by side, to about 1.5 m inside the service line (y ≈ 15.5). You're arriving as their soft ball arrives. The last caption: "Their volley has to go up. Move in together." That leads into step 4, When to take the net.
- **When not to:** if the ball comes out low and fast and you're cramped, play the Beginner lob. The chiquita needs a comfortable ball.
