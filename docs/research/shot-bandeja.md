## Where sources differ, and what we chose

- **How much slice.** Sources 7 and 8 say slice is essential ("Slice is not optional", 8). Source 13 says don't overdo it: hit about 60% flat, 40% slice, or the ball dies short and never reaches the glass. Source 12 says "a little bit of slice or flat". **We chose:** swing high to low with a slightly open face, which gives *some* slice. We tell the reader not to chop under the ball, because it should still carry deep to the glass.
- **Exact contact height.** Most say about forehead or eye level (2, 5, 6, 11). Source 9 says as high as you can, just below full stretch. Source 13 says a little higher than the eyes, because letting it drop makes you hit up into the net. Source 10 says between the shoulder and the head. Source 11 warns that contact straight above you gives a steep path that bounces up off the glass. **We chose:** about forehead height, in front and to the side. Don't let it drop below your head, and don't reach up to full stretch above you.
- **Default target.** Source 7 makes the centre the default. Sources 4, 5, 9 and 13 make cross-court into the corner or side glass the default (source 5: the front panel of the side glass up to the fence). Source 13 uses the middle when the opponent is stepping in to block. **We chose:** cross-court into the corner as the default, the middle as the second option. Down the line is left out of the beginner steps.
- **Step size going back.** Source 8 says small side steps. Source 13 says big steps from the net to the bandeja spot (small steps are for defending). Source 11 says side-step, because it's faster than backpedalling. **We chose:** "quick steps back, side-on", with no size given.
- **Jump or keep your feet down.** Source 10 says keep your feet on the ground unless you're out of position. Source 9 says jump on a very deep lob, to get behind the ball and land moving forward. Source 13 says it's personal: be stable if you're in time, and jump if the lob is long. **We chose:** feet set for the steps. A small hop forward only goes in "advanced" notes, if anywhere.
- **Grip.** Sources 7, 8 and 9 give continental only. Source 10 also allows an eastern forehand grip. **We chose:** continental.
- **Two techniques.** The Padel School (video `ogYbQtN6lNk`, description) teaches a "chest" bandeja first and an "arm" bandeja (close to a vibora) later. Source 5 shows Bela's chest-driven version as the low-risk one. **We chose:** only the simple chest version, a "high forehand volley". The vibora is a separate shot.
- **Where on court you take it.** Source 13 gives a landmark: between "the second panel and the line". Shorter than that is a smash; if the ball bounces beyond the line, let it bounce. Which panel he means isn't clear, so we don't use it as an anchor. **We chose:** "around the service line" (8, 13, 10). **Owner to check** if you want a landmark here.
