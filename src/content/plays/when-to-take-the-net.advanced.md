---
play: Chiquita at their feet, then close in
shots: [Chiquita, Volley]
---

When they're tight on the net, a chiquita is the other way up: a soft, low ball that just clears the net and dips at the feet of the player on the diagonal. From down there they can only lift it, and a ball coming up is one you can volley.

It gives you less time than a lob, so go only as far as it lets you. Move in fast together as it dips, stop and split step as they reach it, even if that's just inside your service line. Then keep coming: take their lifted ball early, in the air, and close the rest of the way to the second post behind it.

It's the harder way up. The ball has to be soft and low, and it usually starts a quick exchange of volleys rather than handing you the net.

### When not to

If the chiquita sits up, stay back. They'll volley it down at your feet.

### Watch out for

- **Still running as they hit.** Stop and split step as they hit, so you're ready to go whichever way their ball does. Then go again.
- **Stopping for good in the gap.** The split step is a pause, not a place to stay. Once their ball comes up, keep moving in.
