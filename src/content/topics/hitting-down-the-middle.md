---
title: Hitting down the middle
kind: situation
summary: You're at the net with a ball to volley, and the space between the two of them is the safest target you have.
draft: true
spot: [6.2, 11.3]
---

- **Aim between them.** A ball down the middle asks them a question: whose is it? Either they both go for it and get in each other's way, or they both leave it, or one of them takes it late.
- **It's the biggest target on the court.** The middle is 5 m from both side walls, so it leaves you the most room for error. A ball that hits their wall or fence before it bounces loses you the point, and down the middle that's hard to do.
- **At the net, make it your first choice.** When you're not sure where to volley, go down the middle. From there their reply has little angle to work with, so it tends to come back through the middle, where you're waiting.
- **From the back, keep it low.** When they're both at the net, the middle only works as a slow, low ball at their feet. A slow, high lob down the middle is the easiest ball they'll get to smash. If you do lob there, make it faster, not higher.
- **Agree who takes your own middle.** They'll aim there too. Settle it before you play: usually it's whoever has the forehand in the middle, which for two right-handers is the player on the left. Then move as a pair, so no gap opens up between you.
