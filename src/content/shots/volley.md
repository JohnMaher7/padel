## When to use it

Once your team is at the net, most balls that reach you get volleyed. Stand level with the second post along the side (the net post counts as the first), about 2 m from the net. Step in when their ball is dropping at their feet, and back when a lob is coming.

Most volleys don't win the point. They keep the ball low and deep until an easy one comes along to finish. There's glass behind the other team, so a hard volley often comes back: where you put it matters more than how hard you hit it.

How much you swing depends on the ball. A fast one needs only a block, because its pace does the work. A slower, higher one gives you time to swing a little more and attack. And you can't volley a serve: it has to bounce first.

## Step by step

1. **Get ready.** Continental grip, so one grip works on both sides. Racket up in front of you at about chest height, knees bent, on your toes.
2. **Split step as they hit.** A small, low hop as their racket meets the ball, not a big jump.
3. **Turn your shoulders.** The racket goes back no further than your shoulder.
4. **Step in.** Step towards the ball with the opposite foot (your left, on a right-hander's forehand), so your weight moves into the court. With no time, a small step on the same side is fine, as long as your shoulders still turn.
5. **Meet it in front.** Contact in front of your body, with a firm wrist and the racket head above it. For a low ball, bend your knees, not your back.
6. **Punch, don't swing.** A short push forward with a firm, nearly flat racket face. A little underspin comes on its own; don't chop down. Finish short, towards your target.
7. **Aim low and deep, then reset.** At their feet or down the middle, past their service line. Under pressure, send it back where it came from. Then get straight back to ready.

## Watch out for

- **Waiting with the racket down.** Keep it up and stay on your toes, even when the ball is going to your partner.
- **A big backswing.** It makes you late. A volley is a push, not a full stroke.
- **Standing in the wrong place.** Right on the net, you're easy to lob. Too far back, you're volleying balls off your feet.
- **Flicking the wrist on a low ball.** Don't drop the racket head and flick it up. Get down with your knees and keep the head up.
